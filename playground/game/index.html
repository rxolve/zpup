One scene, eight directions

Everything here was drawn by an AI model with Zpup: it wrote each object once, as a small 3D scene in JSON, and Zpup drew that scene from eight sides. The eight pictures cannot disagree, because they are one object.

The characters also move. A pose is a short list of changes to the scene, a few joint angles, and every pose is drawn from the same eight sides: a dog and a robot that walk, a person whose knees and elbows bend, a bird that flaps, and a cat that sits, crouches and jumps.

Move: arrow keys or WASD, or click where the dog should go. Turn a prop: click it. Make the cat jump: click it, or walk up to it.

The sheets

Each is one PNG with a frame list beside it, as render_sprites writes them. The same two files load in Phaser and PixiJS as they are. The scene and the poses are the JSON the model wrote, each character in one session. A character's sheet has a row for each direction and a column for each pose.

What this is not

The motion is in steps: each pose is a still picture, and this page plays them in turn. Nothing is drawn between two poses. There are no textures and no perspective, and the look is one flat style. The game around the sprites, the paths and the timing, was written by hand.
